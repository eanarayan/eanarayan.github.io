About

Particle physicist, detector researcher, and lifelong learner.

I am an experimental particle physicist and postdoctoral researcher at Southern Methodist University in Dallas, where I collaborate with the ATLAS experiment at CERN.

My current research spans both physics analysis and detector development. I contribute to searches for Higgs-boson pair production, particularly the channel in which one Higgs boson decays into two bottom quarks and the other into two tau leptons. This rare process gives us an important way to study the properties of the Higgs boson and how it interacts with itself.

I also work on the Gigabit Cable Receiver, or GBCR, a custom chip being developed for the upgraded ATLAS pixel detector. In simple terms, the GBCR helps restore the quality of high-speed electrical signals as data travel from the innermost part of the detector to downstream readout electronics.

Before joining SMU, I completed my Ph.D. in Experimental Particle Physics at the University of New Mexico. My doctoral research focused on B-physics and detector development, including direct contributions to ATLAS's precision measurement of the lifetime of the neutral B meson.

I have been hard of hearing since birth and use hearing aids. Captions are an important part of how I communicate: I use them extensively for online meetings and, through my phone, during many in-person conversations as well.

Beyond particle physics, I am interested in emerging technologies, artificial intelligence, scientific communication, and how new computational tools can support research, teaching, and higher education.

Selected Work

Physics · Instrumentation · Computing

01

Higgs-boson pair production

I work on searches for events in which two Higgs bosons are produced together, focusing on the bbττ final state.

The analysis combines precision particle reconstruction, statistical methods, and machine-learning techniques to identify an extremely rare signal among much larger backgrounds.

ATLAS briefing →
02

Detector electronics

I work on the Gigabit Cable Receiver ASIC for the ATLAS Inner Tracker upgrade.

The chip helps recover the integrity of high-speed signals traveling from the pixel detector near the collision point toward the detector's data-acquisition electronics.

03

Precision B-physics

During my Ph.D., I worked on precision measurements of B mesons using ATLAS data, including a measurement of the neutral B-meson lifetime that achieved unprecedented precision.

Featured Result

Precision measurements of the neutral B meson.

As part of my doctoral research at the University of New Mexico, I contributed directly to an ATLAS measurement of the lifetime of the neutral B meson.

The measurement achieved the highest precision available for this quantity at the time and provides an important test of our understanding of heavy-flavor physics.
